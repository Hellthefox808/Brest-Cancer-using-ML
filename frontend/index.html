Wisconsin FNA Cytological Grading Reference

The Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC) metrics evaluate Fine Needle Aspirate (FNA) biopsies. Each property is graded on an integer scale from 1 (normal/benign) to 10 (most aggressive/malignant):

1. Clump Thickness:

Benign cells tend to group in single layers; cancerous cells cluster in chaotic multi-layered clumps.

2. Uniform Cell Size:

Malignant cells show marked variation in size, whereas normal cells display uniform dimensions.

3. Uniform Cell Shape:

Cancer cells exhibit pleomorphism, differing wildly in shape and perimeter geometry.

4. Marginal Adhesion:

Normal cells stick tightly together. Malignant cells lose intercellular adhesion, facilitating invasion.

5. Single Epithelial Size:

Significantly enlarged epithelial cells frequently indicate malignancy or dysplasia.

6. Bare Nuclei:

Bare nuclei without cytoplasmic boundaries are strongly correlated with malignant tumors.

7. Bland Chromatin:

Benign nuclei have uniform, fine chromatin; malignant cells have coarse, clumped chromatin.

8. Normal Nucleoli:

Normal cells show small or invisible nucleoli; active tumor cells develop enlarged nucleoli.

9. Mitoses:

High mitotic index reflects elevated pathological cellular replication and rapid tumor growth.
